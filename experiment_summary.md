- **Overall objective**
  - Tested whether large language models exhibit self-preference when judging medical responses generated by their own model family.
  - Compared self-preference in two settings: single-turn specialist question answering and multi-turn patient-physician conversations.
  - Examined whether self-preference changes as conversation length increases.

- **Models evaluated**
  - GPT-5.5
  - Claude Sonnet 5
  - Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite
  - Qwen 3.6 35B, run locally with Ollama
  - Each model served as both a response generator and a judge.

- **Datasets and sampling**
  - Sampled 125 clinical questions from the Real-POCQi dataset for the single-turn experiment.
  - Sampled 125 patient scenarios from the ChatDoctor-HealthCareMagic-100k dataset for the multi-turn experiment.
  - Chose 125 scenarios per setting based on a power analysis targeting 90% power at a two-sided significance level of 0.05.
  - The power analysis estimated that approximately 119 paired observations were needed to detect a score-level effect of d = 0.30 and approximately 114 observations were needed to detect a 65% own-pick rate against a 50% null rate.

- **Single-turn experiment**
  - Prompted each model to answer every Real-POCQi question as a specialist in the question's associated medical specialty.
  - Generated one response per model for each of the 125 questions, producing 500 responses in total.
  - Compared responses across all six unique pairs of the four models.
  - Used both models in each pair as judges in separate evaluation runs.
  - Collected 1,500 pairwise judgments: 6 model pairs x 2 judges x 125 questions.

- **Multi-turn experiment**
  - Used each sampled HealthCareMagic patient query as the opening patient message.
  - Had each physician model independently generate an eight-turn conversation for every scenario.
  - Alternated physician turns from the model under study with patient turns from a standardized GPT-5.5 patient simulator.
  - Produced 500 eight-turn conversations: 125 scenarios x 4 physician models.
  - Derived two-, four-, and six-turn versions by truncating each eight-turn conversation.
  - Repeated pairwise judging independently at conversation lengths of 2, 4, 6, and 8 turns.
  - Collected 6,000 pairwise judgments: 6 model pairs x 2 judges x 4 conversation lengths x 125 scenarios.

- **Pairwise judging procedure**
  - Matched the two responses or conversations in each comparison by scenario ID.
  - Presented the original medical prompt and the two outputs to the judge as Response A and Response B.
  - Concealed generator identities from the judge.
  - Randomized A/B response order for each scenario with a fixed seed so both judges in a model pair saw the same ordering.
  - Asked each judge to score both outputs and independently select Response A, Response B, or a tie.
  - Evaluated each output from 0 to 5 on faithfulness, completeness, safety, clarity, and conciseness.
  - Calculated the overall score as the mean of the five criterion scores.
  - Kept the numeric scoring decision independent from the judge's head-to-head preference decision.

- **Score-level self-preference experiment**
  - Held a model's generated response or conversation fixed and compared the score assigned by its own-family judge with the score assigned by the other model's judge.
  - Defined SP-Bias as the own-family judge's overall score minus the outside judge's overall score, averaged across scenarios.
  - Interpreted positive SP-Bias as the model judging its own output more generously and negative SP-Bias as the outside judge assigning the higher score.
  - Used a paired one-sample t-test to test whether the mean within-output score difference was different from zero.
  - Reported p-values and 95% confidence intervals.

- **Decision-level self-preference experiment**
  - Measured how often a judge selected its own model family's output in a direct head-to-head comparison.
  - Excluded ties from statistical testing.
  - Used a two-sided binomial test against a 50% own-pick null rate.
  - Classified a result as evidence of self-preference only when the own-pick rate exceeded 50% and the p-value was below 0.05.
  - Treated this measure as potentially reflecting both judge self-preference and genuine differences in output quality.

- **Total experimental scale**
  - Generated 500 single-turn specialist responses and 500 eight-turn patient-physician conversations.
  - Conducted 7,500 identity-blind, order-randomized pairwise judgments across both settings.
