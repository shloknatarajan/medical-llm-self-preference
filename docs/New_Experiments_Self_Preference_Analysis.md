# Self-preference analysis of the expanded medical LLM experiments

## Summary

This analysis uses only the completed expanded experiments saved in `data/`.
The legacy pairwise experiments are outside its scope.

The primary analysis finds consistent matched self-preference in both tasks:

- In Real-POCQi, judges rank their own exact-model answer **1.219 positions
  higher** on average than other judges rank that same answer (95% CI 1.177 to
  1.261 positions higher; 620 questions).
- In MedSP1000, the corresponding effects are **0.518, 0.457, 0.502, and
  0.613 positions higher** at 2, 4, 6, and 8 visible turns, respectively (200
  questions at every length).
- The Real-POCQi effect remains when the rubric is removed: the no-rubric
  direct-ranking estimate is 1.263 positions (95% CI 1.164 to 1.361) on the
  100-question sensitivity sample.
- Conversation length matters jointly, but the pattern is **not monotonic**.
  The evidence does not support a simple claim that self-preference steadily
  increases with every additional pair of turns.

These are matched *same-model affinity* effects under blinded generator
identity. They do not establish that a judge consciously recognizes its own
answer.

## Primary estimand

For question (q) and exact model (j), let (r_{kqj}) be the rank that judge
(k) assigns to the answer generated by model (j). Lower ranks are better.
The matched self-preference contrast is

\[
D_{jq}=r_{jqj}-\frac{1}{J-1}\sum_{k\ne j}r_{kqj}.
\]

Thus, (D_{jq}<0) means that judge (j) ranks its own answer more favorably
than the outside judges rank the **same answer to the same question**. Matching
on the exact answer removes general generator quality as an explanation: a
strong answer will tend to receive good ranks from both its own and outside
judges.

This estimand is preferable to either a raw own-answer rank or an own-win rate.
A model can have an excellent raw rank because its answer is genuinely strong,
and it can have a poor raw rank while still moving its answer upward relative to
other judges.

## Position adjustment and uncertainty

Candidate order has a measurable association with rank. Ranks are therefore
adjusted with an additive model containing:

- a fixed effect for each judge-by-generator cell; and
- categorical effects for presentation positions 1 through 8 in Real-POCQi or
  1 through 6 in MedSP1000.

The fitted position contribution is removed before constructing the matched
contrast. Treating position categorically avoids assuming a linear stepwise
effect. Adjustment barely changes the pooled estimates, indicating that random
position imbalance does not explain the result.

For judge-specific intervals, questions are the independent units. For pooled
intervals, the correlated judge effects are first averaged within each
question, and the standard error is then calculated across questions. Reported
95% intervals are normal-approximation intervals over questions. Judge-specific
families of tests use Holm correction; substantive interpretation emphasizes
effect sizes and intervals rather than a binary significance threshold.

## Real-POCQi results

### Primary matched rank effect

| Judge | Rank effect | 95% CI |
|---|---:|---:|
| GPT-5.6 Sol | -2.939 | [-3.041, -2.836] |
| GPT-5.6 Terra | -2.224 | [-2.325, -2.123] |
| Gemini 3.7 Flash | -1.236 | [-1.317, -1.155] |
| Claude Opus 5 | -0.969 | [-1.019, -0.919] |
| Qwen 3.5 122B | -0.730 | [-0.852, -0.608] |
| Gemini 3.1 Pro | -0.683 | [-0.790, -0.575] |
| Claude Sonnet 5 | -0.647 | [-0.763, -0.530] |
| Qwen 3.8 27B | -0.324 | [-0.419, -0.228] |
| **Pooled across judges within question** | **-1.219** | **[-1.261, -1.177]** |

Every judge's estimate is negative, every judge-specific interval excludes
zero, and all eight corresponding tests remain below 0.05 after Holm
correction. The effect is highly heterogeneous: Sol's matched
uplift is roughly nine times the Qwen 27B uplift. A single pooled number should
therefore not be interpreted as a universal model behavior.

The unadjusted pooled estimate is -1.219, essentially identical to the adjusted
estimate. Position itself matters—especially being shown first—but randomized
position imbalance does not account for the matched self-preference result.

### Calibration-aware rubric-score sensitivity

Absolute rubric scales differ substantially across judges. To avoid treating a
generally generous judge as self-preferring, the five-axis rubric sum is
standardized within every judge-question candidate list. The standardized
score is then position-adjusted and compared for the same answer across its own
and outside judges. Signs are oriented so that negative again denotes
self-preference.

The pooled standardized-score effect is **-0.440 within-list SD units** (95% CI
[-0.460, -0.420]). All eight judge-specific effects are negative. Agreement in
direction between explicit ranks and normalized scores makes an
absolute-score-calibration explanation unlikely.

### No-rubric direct-ranking sensitivity

On the deterministic 100-question direct-ranking sample:

- the pooled matched effect is **-1.263 positions** (95% CI [-1.361, -1.164]);
- seven of eight judge-specific intervals exclude zero; Qwen 27B is the
  exception at -0.155 positions (95% CI [-0.346, 0.037]);
- direct and rubric-condition rankings agree on **91.5% of candidate pairs**,
  select the same first-place answer in **87.1%** of judge-question cells, and
  produce the identical complete ordering in **16.1%**; and
- the direct-minus-combined change in matched self-preference is -0.071
  positions (95% CI [-0.150, 0.009], nominal (p=0.081)).

Removing the explicit rubric therefore changes fine ordering but does not
remove the main self-preference pattern.

### Primary matched rank effects by transcript length

| Visible turns | Rank effect | 95% CI | Normalized-rubric sensitivity |

| 2 | -0.518 | [-0.585, -0.452] | -0.309 [-0.348, -0.269] |

All four pooled rank effects and all four calibration-aware rubric

Judge-specific rank effects show substantial variation over length:

| Judge | 2 turns | 4 turns | 6 turns | 8 turns |

| Claude Opus 5 | -0.153 | -0.218 | -0.423 | -0.445 |
| Claude Sonnet 5 | -0.233 | -0.378 | -0.549 | -0.472 |
| Gemini 3.1 Pro | -0.791 | -0.554 | -0.436 | -0.545 |
| Gemini 3.7 Flash | -0.572 | -0.600 | -0.428 | -0.645 |
| GPT-5.6 Sol | -0.536 | -0.487 | -0.539 | -0.938 |
| GPT-5.6 Terra | -0.826 | -0.508 | -0.637 | -0.632 |

No judge exhibits a simple shared trajectory. For example, Opus becomes more
self-preferring with length, Gemini Pro is strongest at two turns, and Sol has
a marked increase only at eight turns.

### Repeated-measures length analysis

Because the four conditions are truncations of the same 200 trajectories, they
must not be analyzed as independent groups. We first average the six correlated
judge effects within each question and then compare the four lengths within
question.

A three-dimensional Hotelling test of the 4-minus-2, 6-minus-2, and
8-minus-2 contrasts rejects equality across lengths:

\[
F(3,197)=3.972,\quad p=0.0089.
\]

The individual paired contrasts are:

| Contrast | Change in signed effect | 95% CI | Nominal p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 minus 2 turns | +0.061 | [-0.024, +0.146] | 0.158 |
| 6 minus 2 turns | +0.016 | [-0.077, +0.110] | 0.729 |
| 8 minus 2 turns | -0.094 | [-0.187, -0.002] | 0.045 |

Negative changes mean stronger self-preference at the later length. Although
the 8-minus-2 contrast is nominally below 0.05, it does not survive Holm
correction across these three baseline contrasts. The adjacent 8-minus-6 change
is -0.111 positions (95% CI [-0.189, -0.033], nominal (p=0.0054)). Overall,
the defensible conclusion is that self-preference varies with visible length
and is strongest at eight turns in the pooled data—not that it rises
monotonically from two through eight turns.

## Interpretation

The new experiments provide strong evidence of relative exact-model affinity:
when the answer is held fixed, its own model ranks it more favorably than the
other models do. This conclusion is robust to presentation-position adjustment,
within-judge score calibration, removal of the explicit rubric, and two
different medical evaluation settings.

The result should not be described as raw score inflation or as a model merely
choosing a genuinely better answer. Those interpretations are addressed by
matching on the same answer and comparing judges. It is also safer to say
"same-model affinity" than conscious self-recognition: identities were hidden,
so the effect may arise from shared style, reasoning conventions, favored
evidence, or family-specific quality criteria.

## Limitations

- There is one saved generation per model-question cell. The analysis measures
  affinity for these realized answers and cannot separate stable model behavior
  from generation-specific idiosyncrasy.
- There is one successful judging realization per logical cell. Question-level
  intervals quantify variation across questions, not run-to-run stochasticity
  of the judges.
- Rankings are mutually dependent within each candidate list. Averaging judges
  within question addresses clustering for the pooled estimand, but the normal
  intervals remain large-sample approximations.
- Exact-model and broader family affinity are difficult to separate completely,
  particularly for the two-model OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Qwen families.
- Response length and stylistic differences may mediate the effect. They should
  be treated as possible mechanisms, not automatically "controlled away" if
  the scientific target is total same-model affinity.
- The analysis is exploratory and was not preregistered. P-values should not be
  presented as confirmatory evidence independent of the effect sizes and
  sensitivity checks.

## Recommended paper-level claim

> Across completed blinded Real-POCQi and MedSP1000 experiments, LLM judges
> ranked answers from their own exact model more favorably than other judges
> ranked the same answers. The effect persisted after presentation-position
> adjustment and in calibration-aware and no-rubric sensitivity analyses, but
> varied substantially by judge. In multi-turn conversations it was present at
> every tested length and strongest at eight turns in pooled analysis, without
> a monotonic increase from two to eight turns.
